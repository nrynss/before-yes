## Findings

| ID | Sev | Finding | Pin |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | M | `DetectKind` text scoring is unbounded substring counting, so it overrides structural URL signals and misfires on common words. `strings.Count(text, "steam")` counts "livestream", "mainstream" and "streamers"; "console" counts any mention. One review page that lists platforms ("not on Xbox, PlayStation, Steam, Nintendo Switch") outvotes a direct Google Play listing, and the Android title gets the console plan, which neither excludes Play nor drops Play hits. | Throwaway test (deleted after): hits {`play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=x`, title "Some App - Apps on Google Play", content "Android"} plus one review hit naming console, PC game, Xbox, PlayStation, Steam, Nintendo Switch → `console_pc`. |
| L1 | L | A YouTube title is recognised only by channel-path URLs, "youtube" in the asked title, or "youtube channel / youtuber / subscribers" in hit text. Hits that are `youtube.com/watch` videos, or titled "… - YouTube", score nothing, so a channel whose hits are videos falls to the default `console_pc`. | Throwaway test: "MrBeast" with one hit `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1` titled "MrBeast video" → `console_pc`. |
| L2 | L | `GatherVersion` hashes `Plan` drawn with and without a host, but not `PlanFor` for the android, console and YouTube kinds. A change to the rating-board or channel query, or the Play exclusion, does not change the evidence key unless someone bumps `PlanVersion` by hand, which the T3.4 round-2 L1 fix set out to avoid. | `internal/pipeline/pipeline.go` `GatherVersion`: loops `Plan("Sample Title", host)` only. |
