## Findings

### M1. A setting is backed by any generic 3-word run, so an invented control still stands
`carries(q, setting, 3, -1)` accepts any 3-word run of the setting that holds one word of 3+ letters that is not glue. Common phrases pass, such as "made in the app" or "in the app". Probe (scratch test, not committed):
- Mitigation quote: "All purchases made in the app are listed on the receipt page."
- Setting: "Turn on the spending PIN made in the app". Reason: "A PIN exists: purchases made in the app are blocked."
- Result: `ok_with_setting` stands. The parent is told to turn on a PIN that no quote mentions.

This is the round-1 H2 failure still getting through. It is not a fundamental limit. Settings are short and name a control, so the rule can be strict without a prompt change. One option: every content word of the setting must appear in the quote, or the setting must appear whole after normalisation. Glue such as "turn on" can be stripped first. Re-run replay afterwards. No recorded setting survives today, so the replay should not move.

### L1 (accepted). Invented claims can carry a pasted run of a real quote
A reason such as "It sells data to advertisers; a parent says my daughter loves building worlds." keeps a `flag` on a benign risk-stance quote. Post-hoc string checks can't tell whether a claim follows from its quote, and the brief rules out a prompt change. The cited finding must still be verified, in the same area, with a risk stance from the reader. Its quote is shown on the card next to the reason, so the parent sees the real words. Accepted for this task's scope. A judge prompt that requires quoting, or a separate entailment check, is the follow-up.

### L2 (accepted). `normWords` does not apply NFC
`finding.Normalize` applies NFC to quotes, but `normWords` does not. A reason with a decomposed "é" fails to match a composed quote (probe: "café" vs "cafe\u0301" falls to unknown). This fails safe, as a false negative toward unknown. Curly and straight apostrophes and all punctuation fold correctly. Short quotes are handled: one shorter than 4 words matches when it appears whole ("strangers can message kids" stands). Accepted. Calling `norm.NFC.String` in `normWords` is a one-line follow-up.
