Judgment calls the author listed:

- **(a) An unmeasured reply's `Price` is the client's estimate.** Faithful. The task says the meter settles an unmeasured reply at the estimate; carrying the same figure in `Price` cannot overcharge a caller that ignores `Measured`, and `Measured: false` still marks it. A caller without a meter is better served by the estimate than by zero.
- **(b) All-zero usage is unmeasured.** Faithful. An all-zero pair cannot be told apart from a host that reported nothing, and pricing it at zero would record a possibly paid reply as free. One non-zero count is measured, so a partial report is still priced. The only cost is that a genuinely free reply on a paid host is charged the estimate, which for the two configured Token Factory roles is under a tenth of a cent.
- **(c) A decodable 200 with no `choices` is retried on the smaller 5xx budget.** Faithful to the task text: it forbids retrying only a non-429 4xx or an undecodable 200, and this body decodes. The billing caveat is recorded as L3.
- **(d) `Estimate` exported.** Faithful and useful. The draft consumer computes the same reservation in `pipeline.chat` and passes it to `Meter.Spend`, so an exported `Estimate` lets that arithmetic move into the client when the draft is lifted; today the two agree exactly.

Attempts that failed to break it:

- Charging zero for a paid call: all four "no measurement" shapes (absent, null, empty, all-zero) and both negative shapes are unmeasured and carry a positive estimate.
- Re-asking a billed 200: four undecodable shapes each stopped at one request.
- Retrying a permanent 4xx: eight statuses, one request each.
- Losing sibling `extra` keys across a per-call override: the nested switch flipped and `top_k` survived.
- Beating the pacer with concurrent starts: the two arrivals were one interval apart.
- Drifting from the draft consumer: the interface, the constructor shape and every field read compile against the branch.

For T2.1b and later:

- The pacer window is named `provider/model`, so two roles on one host pace independently. If Token Factory's per-minute cap is per account rather than per model, the reader and judge can together exceed it. T2.1b should record which the cap is.
- `http.Client{Timeout: 3 * time.Minute}` is fixed and not configurable, while the draft judge's thinking-on pass asks for 16 000 output tokens. T2.1b records the latencies, and T3.3 should notice if a thinking call approaches three minutes.
- An error member inside a 200 is always a retryable 502 whatever its `code`: measured with `code: 402`, it became a 502 and made two requests. That is what the task asks for; T2.1b could record whether OpenRouter ever uses the member for a permanent condition.
