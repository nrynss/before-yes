| M1 | M | Treating bold or a link as a title signal lets through the advice and section markup that parent-guide and listicle pages use most. This search ("safer … for a 9 year old") returns exactly those pages. Probe results from `listNames`: `1. **Turn on parental controls** / 2. **Disable chat** in settings / 3. **Set a time limit**` gives all three. `## **Why we like it** / ## **Our verdict** / ## **Pros and cons**` gives all three. `1. **Note:** check age / 2. **Tip:** use family sharing` gives `[Note, Tip]`. `1. [Download the app](x) / 2. [Open settings](y)` gives both. `plausibleName` cannot catch any of them: each starts with a capital, has no "?", and has six words or fewer. Each one takes a slot, costs a full `Collect`, and can be shown as a refused alternative. This is the round-2/3 cost and slot problem again, now through markup. To fix it, markup alone should not be enough. Ways to do that: (a) require both markup and corroboration, or corroboration alone, for entries on a page with no ranked numbering; (b) for a bold-only heading, require numbering or corroboration; (c) reject a marked lead that ends in ":" (labels such as "Note:" and "Tip:") and a bold span that is followed by more text on the same line unless the entry is numbered. Pin the fix with these four pages. Corroboration-only is the safest option: the accepted trade-off already lets unmarked list pages yield nothing. | Throwaway probe (deleted) |
